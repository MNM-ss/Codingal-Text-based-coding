~Life Cycle of a Plant~

The life cycle of a plant describes the stages it goes through from the beginning of its life to reproduction and the start of a new generation. It is a continuous process that ensures the survival of plant species. Although the details may vary slightly among different types of plants, most flowering plants follow a similar pattern that includes seed, germination, growth, reproduction, and seed dispersal.

The cycle begins with a seed. Inside the seed is a tiny embryo along with stored food that helps the plant begin to grow. When the seed is placed in suitable conditions—usually with enough water, air, and the right temperature—it begins the process of germination. During germination, the seed absorbs water, swells, and the outer covering breaks open. A small root, called the radicle, grows downward into the soil to absorb water and nutrients, while a shoot grows upward toward the light.

As the plant continues to grow, it develops roots, stems, and leaves. The roots anchor the plant and absorb water and minerals from the soil. The stem supports the plant and transports nutrients, while the leaves carry out photosynthesis, the process by which plants make their own food using sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water. This stage is known as the vegetative stage, where the plant focuses on growing larger and stronger.

Once the plant reaches maturity, it enters the reproductive stage. In flowering plants, flowers develop and play a key role in reproduction. Pollination occurs when pollen from the male part of a flower is transferred to the female part, often by wind, water, or insects. After pollination and fertilization, seeds begin to form inside fruits. These seeds are eventually dispersed by wind, animals, or other natural methods, starting the life cycle again.
